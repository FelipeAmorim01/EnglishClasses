## His level

**Known, as of the diagnostic on 15 September 2026: a false beginner.** Felipe's
read after the class — *"he can speak the basics well, but doesn't have a very
sophisticated vocabulary."*

So: not an absolute beginner, and not someone who freezes. The core grammar is
there and comes out. **The gap is range** — words, collocations and the
connective tissue that turns single correct sentences into an answer.

What that means for planning:

- **Stop hedging across three levels.** Pitch at one.
- Grammar is still the spine — this is not Raffa's track, and the 60–80 item
  vocabulary target still does not apply here. But a block that only re-drills
  something he already says correctly is a wasted block.
- The productive move is to **carry vocabulary in on the back of grammar**:
  false-friend fix cards, sequencing words, collocations attached to whatever
  structure is being taught.
- He reached blocks 1–5 in a single hour. That is the pace to plan for.

Still unrecorded: how much of the BBC homework he actually understood, which is
the second read on his listening level. Worth asking at the start of lesson 1.

## Lesson 0 — Getting Started
`lesson_plan_0.html` · 15 September 2026 · diagnostic

Stages: Warm-up → Your First Toolkit → The Question Machine → Wrap-up

Built with more material than one hour can reach, deliberately, so blocks carry
over. A realistic first hour is blocks 1–3.

**Warm-up:** the five-sentence ladder, then greetings — hello / how are you /
answering / meeting someone / goodbye (26 items).

**Toolkit**, six numbered blocks, opening with the who-speaks legend:

1. **Staying in English** — *Sorry, I don't understand* · *Can you repeat that,
   please?* · *Can you say it slowly?* · *How do you say this in English?* ·
   *How do you spell that?* · *Just a moment* · *Let me think* · *Is this
   correct?* (19 items). The block that makes an English-only class possible.
2. **am / is / are** — full forms vs. contractions, negatives, questions and
   short answers. Six-row vocab table.
3. **Saying who you are** — eight question/answer cards: name, where from, where
   you live, age, do you speak English, married, spelling, phone number. Plus
   **nine sentences to unlearn** as `.fix-card` pairs: *I have thirty-four
   years* · *I have hungry* · *I have sure* · *I am agree* · *I'm with sleep* ·
   *I have five minutes late* · *I like very much* · *actually* for "now" ·
   *pretend* for "plan".
4. **Work** — what do you do / where do you work / who with / do you like it;
   `a` vs `an`; *work at* vs *work in* vs *work for*; 16 jobs, 9 departments,
   11 things at work.
5. **Your day** — present simple, the third-person `-s`, `don't`/`doesn't`,
   `Do`/`Does` questions; frequency adverbs; a working day in order (15 verbs).
6. **Yesterday** — was/were, ten irregular pasts, regular `-ed`, time phrases.
   Marked in prose as only worth opening if 4 and 5 were easy.

**Activity:** the Question Machine — a 26-card no-repeat question deck (two-part
questions), the sentence builder, and one roleplay: *The kitchen at work*, with
Peterson as the new employee and Felipe as a colleague from another team.

**Homework:** BBC Learning English, *English at Work*, episode 1 — The
Interview. https://www.bbc.co.uk/learningenglish/english/features/english-at-work/01-the-interview
(Note: the BBC labels this series Intermediate, which is above where Peterson
probably is. If it lands badly, the lower-intermediate course is the fallback:
https://www.bbc.co.uk/learningenglish/english/course/lower-intermediate)

**Cut during review, do not reintroduce:** level badges (Start here / Next step
/ Stretch), a "Numbers, letters, time" block, the deck's add-on rule strip, a
second roleplay scene, and a three-item homework list.

## Lesson 1 — Last Week
`lesson_plan_1.html` · 16 September 2026 · the day after the diagnostic

Stages: Warm-up → Your Past Toolkit → The Time Machine → Wrap-up

The past simple, properly — block 6 of lesson 0 expanded into a whole page, and
the first lesson built against a known level rather than a guess.

**Warm-up:** a **new five-sentence ladder** pitched at the past, then the *How
was your weekend?* exchange — asking, answering, keeping it going.

**Toolkit**, seven numbered blocks. No legend — each block carries its own
who-speaks cue instead:

1. **was / were** — the one past verb that never takes *did*. Six-row table,
   negatives, short answers. Fix cards: *We was* · *I born in 1990*.
2. **Regular verbs: the -ed ending** — the three sounds, as three chip groups.
   Listen-and-repeat only, said so on the page. Fix cards are pronunciation
   respellings (*work-ed* → *workt*).
3. **The verbs that do their own thing** — eight core irregulars in a table with
   sentences, eighteen more as arrow chips, then **the verb machine** (new
   widget, see page-anatomy).
4. **didn't** — one past per sentence. Five-row table ending on *I wasn't tired*
   to draw the line against block 1. Four fix cards.
5. **Did you …?** — the asking half. Question words, two-word answers, four fix
   cards including *Did she was there?*
6. **When it happened** — four chip groups (yesterday / last / ago / on-in-at),
   time at the end of the sentence. Four fix cards.
7. **Telling it in order** — the vocabulary block. Sequencing chips and three
   question/answer cards modelling a full answer.

Closing the stage, a **Language Tip** (`.rule-box`, the recurring stage 2 closer
from Raffa's pages, first used on this track here): *was / were vs. did /
didn't* — be changes shape itself, every other verb hands the past to `did`, and
one past per sentence. It is the rule blocks 1, 4 and 5 all depend on.

**Activity:** the Time Machine — a 26-card no-repeat deck of two-part past
questions, **Change the sentence** (a 24-card transformation drill whose task
line spells out the change — *Put it in the past* / *Make it negative* / *Make
it a question* — see page-anatomy), and one roleplay: *Monday morning*, Peterson as himself, Felipe
as a colleague who wants the whole weekend.

**Wrap-up:** no ladder bookend. Straight to recall with the page closed, then
homework.

**Homework:** BBC Learning English, *English at Work*, episode 2 — The
Interruption (verified 200). Continues the series from lesson 0.

**Realistic reach:** blocks 1–5 in the hour, on lesson 0's pace. Blocks 6 and 7
are the likely carry — and block 7 is the one that matters most for his actual
gap, so it is worth protecting time for it.

**Cut during review, do not reintroduce on this page:**

- **The staying-in-English block** in the warm-up. Felipe: it was taught in full
  in lesson 0, and the phrases survive in the stage 3 sidebar where he actually
  needs them mid-conversation.
- **The who-speaks legend** at the top of the toolkit. Every block already says
  who speaks, so the legend was a second copy of the same instruction.
- **Nine more to unlearn** — the false-friend fix cards. Not rejected on
  content, only on volume: *"there's already too much on that page."* Parked
  below for a later lesson.
- **The sentence builder.** Felipe: *"too fancy for something that is just for
  reading sentences."* The replacement makes him change a sentence rather than
  read a generated one. The generator, its CSS and `pick()` were all stripped
  from this page; lesson 0 still has them.
- **The ladder bookend** in the wrap-up — *"too repetitive and doesn't add
  anything."* The ladder now appears once, in the warm-up only.

Three of those five cuts contradict rules currently written in `SKILL.md`
(English-only block first on every page, the legend on every page, the ladder as
a wrap-up bookend). **Ask before treating them as standing rules** — as of this
lesson they are decisions about one page.

## Not yet covered

Sequenced roughly by what he needs next, now that the past is done:

- **Going to / want to / would like to** — plans. With the present and the past
  behind him this is the last of the three time frames, and it is what the
  lesson 1 page names as coming up next.
- **Numbers, letters and time** — cut from lesson 0 for time. Zero to a hundred,
  the *thirteen/thirty* stress contrast, spelling out loud (E/I and G/J are the
  ones Portuguese speakers mix up), telling the time, days of the week.
- **There is / there are, and places** — describing an office, a home, a city.
- **Can / can't** — ability and permission, and the workplace uses of both.
- **Comparatives and superlatives** — *bigger*, *better*, *the best*. Cheap
  grammar, and it buys a lot of range for someone whose gap is range.
- **Food, ordering, and shopping** — high-frequency everyday language he will
  actually use.
- **On the phone and on video calls** — a beginner's version: asking someone to
  slow down, spelling names, confirming times.
- **Vocabulary in its own right** — now that the level is known, a page built
  around collocations and phrasal verbs rather than a structure is viable in a
  way it was not before. **The nine false-friend fix cards cut from lesson 1
  belong here**, and they are already written: *assisted* → *watched* · *lost
  the bus* → *missed the bus* · *passed time* → *spent time* · *did a mistake* →
  *made a mistake* · *made a course* → *took a course* · *realized a project* →
  *finished a project* · *very funny* → *really fun* · *stayed in home* →
  *stayed at home* · *went in the cinema* → *went to the cinema*.
