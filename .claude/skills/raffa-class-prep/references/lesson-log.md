## Lesson 1 — Negotiation & Workplace Conflict
`lesson_plan_1.html` · 14 August 2026

Stages: Warm-up → Diplomacy Toolkit → The Negotiation Table → Wrap-up

Diplomatic register: blunt phrasings rewritten as diplomatic ones, pushing back
without conceding, holding a position.

**Idioms:** meet someone halfway · play hardball · read the room · table an issue ·
walk away from the table · give and take

**Chip banks:** Opening · Counteroffer · Standing firm · Closing the deal

**Activity:** two roleplays with hidden counterpart briefs — salary
renegotiation, vendor price increase. Introduced the `.negotiation-layout` +
sticky sidebar pattern that later lessons reuse.

## Lesson 4 — Storytelling & Anecdotes
`lesson_plan_4.html` · 10 September 2026

Stages: Warm-up → The Storyteller's Toolkit → The Story Machine → Wrap-up

The lesson where the vocabulary-first rule was worked out properly. Went through
several revisions to strip out storytelling craft and replace it with language.

**Native-speaker patterns** (three-column table, column 3 read aloud):
`goes` / `is like` as informal reported speech · introductory `this` + noun ·
`be supposed to` / `was going to` for plans that failed · `keep + -ing` for
repeated irritating action · ungradable adjectives with
*absolutely / completely / totally* · vague language (*some guy*, *or something*,
*and stuff like that*)

**Anecdote idioms:** out of the blue · next thing I know · long story short ·
of all people / of all places · it went downhill from there · you had to be there

**Phrasal verbs:** bump into / run into · end up · turn out · show up · get stuck ·
freak out · mess up · kick off · come across · put up with · back out · work out

**Chip banks:** Opening the frame · Signalling the turn · Buying yourself a
second · Surprise · Sympathy / wincing · Pushing for more · Closing it off

**Activities:** a no-repeat reaction drill (a line is read out, he reacts, the
expression is struck through — eighteen available, no repeats), and the Story
Machine (prompt deck + clock + six interruption buttons each labelled with the
grammar it forces, plus a timestamped log).

**Homework:** one episode of *The Moth*, listening for how long the scene-setting
takes.

## Not yet covered

Topic ideas raised and not used, with the language each would carry:

- **Meetings and video calls** — interrupting, reclaiming the floor, disagreeing
  in a group, asking for repetition without sounding lost.
- **Difficult conversations and feedback** — softening without losing meaning,
  delivering bad news, apologising professionally, receiving criticism.
- **Opinions, debate, devil's advocate** — the strength dial from hedged to
  emphatic, conceding a point, *I take your point, but…*
- **Sorting things out in real life** — customer service, landlords, doctors,
  returning things; the escalation ladder from polite to immovable.
- **Taste and recommending** — comparison shortcuts, intensity and enthusiasm
  vocabulary, describing something to someone who has never seen it.
- **When things go wrong at work** — accountability language, escalating upward,
  the vocabulary of uncertainty.
- **Your professional story** — career narrative retold for different audiences.

Early lessons were all work-shaped; he has since asked for material outside the
office, so weight the non-business options accordingly.
