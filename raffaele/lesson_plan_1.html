Stage 02 of 04
Diplomacy Toolkit
Same message, different impact. It's rarely about what you say — it's about how you say it.
The Diplomacy Dial
Too blunt Diplomatic version In context
"That's wrong." I see it a bit differently. "I see it a bit differently — can I explain why?"
"You need to fix this now." Could we prioritize this? "Could we prioritize this before Friday's deadline?"
"I disagree." I'm not sure I fully agree. "I'm not sure I fully agree — can I share another angle?"
"That's not my problem." That falls outside my role, but... "That falls outside my role, but I can point you to Marcos, who handles that."
"No." I'm not able to commit to that right now. "I'm not able to commit to that right now, but let's revisit it next week."
Idiom spotlight — negotiation talk
meet someone halfway
To compromise — each side gives up a little.
"We met halfway on the price."
play hardball
To negotiate aggressively, with no concessions.
"They played hardball during the contract talks."
read the room
To sense the mood before you speak or push an idea.
"He didn't read the room and pushed too hard."
table an issue
To postpone a discussion until later.
"Let's table this and come back to it next meeting."
walk away from the table
To end a negotiation without reaching a deal.
"If the terms aren't fair, be ready to walk away."
give and take
A mutual exchange of compromises.
"Any good deal involves some give and take."
Stage 03 of 04
The Negotiation Table
Two roleplays. You'll play one side — I'll play the other. Use today's language to reach the best deal you can.
How it works

Each scenario has two roles. You'll only see your own role and will negotiate out loud until you reach an agreement or decide to walk away.

Scenario A — Salary Renegotiation Employee vs. Manager
You've been in your role for two years and recently took on extra responsibility.
Your role — Employee
  • You now manage two junior team members.
  • You led a project that saved the company real money last quarter.
  • Market research shows the role pays 15–20% more elsewhere.
  • Your target: a 15% raise.
  • Your walk-away point: 8% minimum, or non-monetary benefits (extra vacation days, remote work, training budget).
Counterpart — Manager
Hidden.
  • Budget only allows 5% raises department-wide this year.
  • You value this employee and don't want to lose them.
  • You can't break salary equity across the team.
  • You CAN offer: a title change, flexible remote days, a training budget, or a formal promotion review in 6 months.
Scenario B — Vendor Price Increase Buyer vs. Supplier
A long-time supplier just announced a price increase, effective next month.
Your role — Buyer
  • Your supplier is raising prices by 12%, citing rising material costs.
  • Your annual budget is already approved and fixed.
  • Your goal: reduce the increase, or get something of equal value in return.
  • You could accept: better payment terms, priority delivery, or a smaller increase.
Counterpart — Supplier
Hidden.
  • Costs genuinely rose — 7% is the minimum increase you need to stay profitable.
  • You'd rather offer extras than lose a long-term client: extended payment terms, priority delivery, or a volume discount.
  • You value the relationship and want to find a deal that works.
Useful language
Opening
I'd like to talk about... Before we start, can I ask... Let's see if we can find something that works for both sides.
Counteroffer
What if we met in the middle at... I could agree to that if you could... Here's an alternative...
Standing firm
I hear you, but I can't go below... That's not something I'm able to change, however... I need to be honest — that doesn't work for me.
Closing the deal
So, to confirm, we're agreeing on... Let's put this in writing. I think we have a deal.

Reflection & Next Steps
Bring it to work
Think of a real negotiation or disagreement waiting for you at work in the next few weeks — a raise, a deadline, a scope change, anything.
  1. In one sentence, what's the situation?
  2. What's your target? What's your walk-away point?
  3. Pick one phrase from today's toolkit. How would you open that conversation? Say it out loud, right now, like you mean it.
