Your opinion toolkit
Three blocks: saying what you think, agreeing and disagreeing, and seeing both sides.
1 · If you ask me — more than "I think"

I think is fine — but these show how sure you are, and whether it's only your view. Most of them start the sentence, with a comma after them. To say no, put the not on think: I don't think it's fair, and the short answer is I don't think so.

What you're saying The words Say it out loud
your view, clearly in my opinion · if you ask me In my opinion, the office is better for meetings. If you ask me, four days a week is enough.
your view, softer I'd say … · it seems to me that … I'd say it's a good idea. It seems to me that people are happier at home.
only for you personally · as far as I'm concerned Personally, I prefer mornings. As far as I'm concerned, the price is fine.
very sure I'm convinced that … · I really believe … I'm convinced that it's the future. I really believe it works.
saying no I don't think … · I don't think so I don't think it's fair. Is it a good idea? I don't think so.
asking What do you think about …? · How do you feel about …? What do you think about the new office? How do you feel about working on Saturdays?
According to me, it's too expensive.
In my opinion, it's too expensive.
according to is for other people: according to my boss, according to the news.
I think it's not a good idea.
I don't think it's a good idea.
The not goes on think. The short answer: I don't think so.
In my point of view, it's fine.
From my point of view, it's fine.
from my point of view — or just in my opinion.
2 · I see your point, but … — agreeing and disagreeing

At work, a direct No, you're wrong sounds rude. So start with their point — that's true, I see your point — and then say but. I couldn't agree more sounds negative, but it means you agree 100%.

What you're saying The words Say it out loud
agree 100% Exactly. · I couldn't agree more. Exactly — that's what I think. I couldn't agree more.
agree You're right. · Good point. You're right, it's too long. Good point.
agree, but … That's true, but … · I see your point, but … That's true, but it takes time. I see your point, but it's expensive.
disagree, softly I'm not so sure about that. · I see it differently. I'm not so sure about that. I see it differently — for me, it works.
disagree, clearly I'm afraid I disagree. · I don't think that's right. I'm afraid I disagree. I don't think that's right.
asking them to agree …, don't you think? It's too expensive, don't you think? It's a good plan, don't you think?
You have reason.
You're right.
right is something you are: you're right, I'm right.
I'm agree with you.
I agree with you.
agree is a verb, like like: I agree, I don't agree.
3 · It depends — seeing both sides

Most real answers aren't yes or no. These let you give both sides and then say where you stand.

What you're saying The words Say it out loud
the answer changes It depends on … It depends on the job. It depends on how much it costs.
two sides on the one hand … on the other hand … On the one hand, it pays well. On the other hand, it's very stressful.
true, but only partly to some extent · in a way To some extent, that's true. In a way, I agree with you.
the real problem the thing is, … It's a good plan. The thing is, nobody has time.
the other side, after your point having said that · even so It's expensive. Having said that, it's worth it. Even so, I'd go.

Language Tip
So do I vs. Neither do I
To agree fast, don't repeat the whole sentence — repeat the helper verb. Use the helper from their sentence: am, have, can, would. If there's no helper, use do, does or did. Then ask: was their sentence yes, or was it not?
They say yes — you say So
So + helper + I.
  • "I love Fridays." — "So do I."
  • "I'm tired today." — "So am I."
  • "I went to bed late." — "So did I."
They say not — you say Neither
Neither + helper + I.
  • "I don't like meetings." — "Neither do I."
  • "I've never been to Japan." — "Neither have I."
  • "I can't cook." — "Neither can I."
The trap

✗ "I don't like Mondays." — "Me too."  →  ✓ "I don't like Mondays." — "Neither do I." / "Me neither."

After a not, me too says the opposite. It agrees with a yes sentence only. And watch the tense: their went has no helper, so it's So did I — never So went I. To disagree, flip it: "I love Fridays." — "Oh, I don't."

Both sides — say all four out loud
  1. "I'm a bit tired today." — "So am I."
  2. "I didn't sleep very well." — "Neither did I."
  3. "I'd love a day off." — "So would I."
  4. "I don't like working on Fridays." — "Really? I do. It's quiet."
