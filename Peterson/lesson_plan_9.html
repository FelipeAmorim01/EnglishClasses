Stage 02 of 04
Your up-to-now toolkit
Three blocks: how long, been or gone, and time that isn't over yet.
1 · I've been working on … — how long, and still going

have been + …ing is for something that started in the past and is still going on — it's the natural way to say how long. for + an amount of time, since + a starting point, as in lesson 7. It also explains what you can see right now: You look tired — have you been working late? Four verbs never take -ing here: know, like, be, and have when it means to own.

What you're saying The words Say it out loud
asking how long How long have you been …ing? How long have you been working here? How long have you been waiting?
an amount of time I've been …ing for … I've been working here for six years. We've been waiting for twenty minutes.
a starting point I've been …ing since … She's been working from home since March. It's been raining since Monday.
again and again I've been trying to … I've been trying to call you all morning. I've been trying to fix it for an hour.
what you can see now You look … — have you been …ing? You look tired — have you been working late? You're out of breath — have you been running?
never with -ing I've known · I've had I've known Paulo for ten years. I've had this phone since 2022.
I'm working here since 2020.
I've been working here since 2020.
With since or for, the sentence reaches back into the past, so it takes have. I'm working is only about now.
How long are you waiting?
How long have you been waiting?
How long are you …? asks about the future: How long are you staying in Rio? Up to now is have you been …ing.
I've been knowing him for years.
I've known him for years.
know, like, be and have (to own) don't take -ing — use have + the third form.
2 · gone or been? — still there, or back again

Both are third forms of go. She's gone to Recife — she's there now, and she isn't back. She's been to Recife — she went, and she came back. That's why Have you ever …? always takes been. Where have you been? is for someone who was away and is here now.

Where they are now The words Say it out loud
still there She's gone to … Ana isn't here — she's gone to Recife. She's gone for the whole week.
back again She's been to … Ana has been to Recife twice. She's been there for work and on holiday.
out for a minute He's just gone out. Paulo has just gone out. He's gone to get a coffee.
nobody left Everyone has gone home. It's late — everyone has gone home. Has Ana gone home too?
you're back I've been away. Sorry I didn't answer — I've been away. I was in Lisbon for a week.
they're back Where have you been? Where have you been? I've been trying to call you all day!
He's been to lunch — he'll be back at two.
He's gone to lunch — he'll be back at two.
He's still out, so it's gone. been would mean he's back already.
Have you ever gone to Japan?
Have you ever been to Japan?
A trip in your life is over — you came back. So it's always been, and always to.
Everyone has gone to home.
Everyone has gone home.
home takes no to: go home, gone home, get home.
3 · so far, lately, in ages — time that isn't over

Lesson 7's rule: a time that isn't over takes have. These are the words that say it isn't over: so far, this week, this year, lately · recently. in ages is a very long time, and goes with haven't. After It's the first time …, it's always I've.

What you're saying The words Say it out loud
up to now so far I've had four meetings so far today. So far, so good.
not over yet this week · this year I've been to Rio twice this year. I haven't had a day off this week.
the last few weeks lately · recently Have you seen any good films lately? I've been really busy recently.
a long time I haven't … in ages. I haven't seen Marcos in ages. I haven't been to the beach in ages.
something new It's the first time I've … It's the first time I've been here. Is it the first time you've tried sushi?
never until now I've never … before. I've never eaten here before. I've never done this before.
It's the first time I come here.
It's the first time I've been here.
You're counting up to now — so after It's the first time, it's I've + the third form.
I don't see her for ages.
I haven't seen her in ages.
A long time up to now takes haven't. in ages or for ages — both are fine.
I didn't see him lately.
I haven't seen him lately.
lately runs right up to now, so it takes have.
Language Tip
I've written vs. I've been writing
Both start in the past and come up to now. The difference is what you're talking about. I've written is the result — what's done, and how many. I've been writing is the activity — how long, and it might not be finished. So ask yourself: am I counting, or saying how long?
I've written — the result, how many
have + third form
  • I've written three emails this morning.
  • She's read the whole report.
  • How many calls have you made today?
I've been writing — the activity, how long
have been + …ing
  • I've been writing emails all morning.
  • She's been reading the report since lunch.
  • How long have you been making calls?
The trap

✗ I've been writing three emails this morning.  →  ✓ I've written three emails this morning.

A number is a result, and a result takes the third form. As soon as you count — three emails, twice, all of them — drop the been …ing. It works the other way too: all morning, for two hours and since lunch are how long, so they want been …ing. And the four verbs from block 1 only ever take the first one: I've known her for years.

Both sides — say all four out loud
  1. I've been writing emails all morning, and I've written twelve.
  2. How long have you been waiting? — Twenty minutes. I've called you three times!
  3. She's been working on the report since Monday, but she hasn't finished it yet.
  4. We've been looking for a new office for months — we've seen ten so far.

Then and now
You talk, I ask

Think about your life five years ago, and your life now. I'll ask questions as you go — the past for back then, and have for what's changed since.

1 · Work
Where you worked five years ago, and what you did. What's changed since then, and what's gotten easier or harder?
2 · Where you live
Where you lived back then. What's gone up, what's gotten better or worse, and what's new?
3 · You
Something you've started, something you've stopped, and something you've gotten better at since then.
4 · Lately
What you've been doing lately that you didn't do five years ago — and how long you've been doing it.
