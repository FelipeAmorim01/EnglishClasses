1 · was / were

The past of am, is and are. Only two forms to learn, and this is the one verb that never uses did. I read the first row of the table, then the italic sentences are yours; the boxed words are mine first and yours after, twice each.

Now Last week Say it out loud

I was you were he / she / it was we were they were
Saying no

Short answers

We, you and they always take were.

2 · Regular verbs: the -ed ending

One spelling, three different sounds. This block is listen and repeat only — I say every word first and you say it back. Do not read them cold, because the spelling will send you the wrong way.

Sounds like a t — after k, p, s, sh, ch, f
worked talked asked watched finished cooked stopped helped
Sounds like a d — after most others
called played listened arrived opened stayed lived answered
A whole extra syllable — only after t and d
wanted started needed waited decided ended visited
work-ed · two syllables
workt · one syllable
Almost every -ed is silent. Say them after me, twice each.
call-ed · two syllables
calld · one syllable
Same again — the -ed is just a d on the end.
wantd · one syllable
wan-ted · two syllables
Only a t or a d before the -ed buys you the extra syllable.

4 · didn't

The one thing to hold on to: didn't already carries the past, so the verb goes straight back to its base form. You never need the past form twice in the same sentence. Table the same way as block 1 — my first row, then the italic sentences are yours. Green lines twice each.

It happened It didn't Say it out loud
I went. I didn't go. I didn't go to the office. I didn't go on Monday, and I didn't go on Friday.
She called. She didn't call. She didn't call me. She didn't call the client, and she didn't call back.
We had time. We didn't have time. We didn't have time. We didn't have time on Tuesday, and we didn't have time today.
It worked. It didn't work. It didn't work. It didn't work yesterday, and it didn't work this morning.
I was tired. I wasn't tired. I wasn't tired. I wasn't tired on Monday, and I wasn't tired at all.

Look at that last row again. was and were make their own negative and never touch didn't — that is the line between block 1 and this one.

I didn't went to the office.
I didn't go to the office.
One past per sentence. didn't has it, so go gives it up.
She didn't called me.
She didn't call me.
The same rule, with a regular verb.
I don't went yesterday.
I didn't go yesterday.
yesterday is in the sentence, so it has to be didn't.
I wasn't go to work.
I didn't go to work.
wasn't and didn't never appear in the same job.
5 · Did you …?

You answer well. This block is the other half — asking. The order never changes: Did, then the person, then the base verb. Italic sentences are yours after my first row; the boxed questions I say first and you say back, twice each.

Telling Asking Say it out loud
You went home. Did you go home? Did you go home? Did you go early? Did you go by car?
He called. Did he call? Did he call? Did he call you, or did he call the office?
They finished. Did they finish? Did they finish? Did they finish on time, and did they send it?
It was good. Was it good? Was it good? Was it useful? Was it a long meeting?
With a question word in front
What did you …? Where did you …? When did you …? Who did you …? Why did you …? How did you …? What time did you …? How long did it …?
Answering in two words
Yes, I did. No, I didn't. Yes, it was. No, it wasn't. Yes, we were. No, I wasn't.
Did you went home?
Did you go home?
Did carries the past, exactly like didn't.
Did she was there?
Was she there?
was asks its own question — move it to the front and drop did.
What you did yesterday?
What did you do yesterday?
Question word, then did, then the person. Always that order.
Where did you went?
Where did you go?
The question word in front changes nothing after it.
6 · When it happened

The past simple wants a time. These go at the end of the sentence — or at the very front for emphasis, but never in the middle. Every chip below is mine first, then yours, twice each.

Yesterday and today
yesterday yesterday morning yesterday afternoon last night this morning
last …
last week last weekend last month last year last Friday last summer
… ago
ten minutes ago two hours ago three days ago a week ago two years ago a long time ago
on · in · at
on Monday on Friday morning in March in 2019 at the weekend
I went yesterday to the office.
I went to the office yesterday.
The time waits until the end. It never sits between the verb and the place.
I worked there since two years.
I worked there two years ago.
ago counts backwards from now, and it comes after the number.
I have gone to the beach last weekend.
I went to the beach last weekend.
Once you name the day, only the past simple fits.
In the last week I was very busy.
Last week I was very busy.
last takes no in and no the.
7 · Telling it in order

Single sentences are not a story. These are the words that join them, and they are what turns three facts into an answer someone wants to listen to. Boxed words as always — I say each one, you say it back twice.

Starting
So, last weekend … First, At first, On Friday night, It started well.
Going on
Then After that, Later, A bit later, Before that, And then
Finishing
In the end, Finally, So that was my weekend. And that was it.
Saying whether it was any good
It was great. It was really fun. It was OK. Nothing special. It was a long day. I was exhausted.

Three real questions, and what a full answer sounds like. The italic answers are yours to read out loud, after I have read the first one.

How was your weekend?
Three parts: what you did, when you did it, and whether it was good.
It was good, thanks. On Saturday I went to my sister's house, and then on Sunday I didn't do much — I stayed at home and watched a film.
What did you do last night?
Say two things, and join them with then or after that.
Nothing special. I finished work at seven, then I made dinner and went to bed early. I was exhausted.
Did you have a good week?
Never just yes. Answer, then give one reason.
Yes, I did. It was busy — we had a big project and I worked late on Tuesday and Wednesday. But we finished it. What about you?
Language Tip
was / were vs. did / didn't
English has two separate machines for the past, and every sentence you say has to pick one. Which one is never a matter of taste — it depends on a single thing: whether the verb is be. Everything else in this lesson follows from getting that one choice right.
be — it changes shape itself
I / he / she / it was
you / we / they were
  • I was tired on Monday.
  • We weren't ready.
  • Was she at the office?
every other verb — did carries it
did / didn't + the base verb
Did + person + the base verb
  • I went home early.
  • I didn't go home early.
  • Did you go home early?
The trap

✗ Did she was there?  →  ✓ Was she there?  ·  ✗ I didn't went.  →  ✓ I didn't go.

One past per sentence. The moment did or didn't appears, it has taken the past for itself and the verb after it goes back to its base form. And be never shares: it makes its own negative and its own question, so did has no work to do in those sentences at all.

One week, four sentences — say all four out loud
  1. I was busy all week.
  2. I wasn't in the office on Friday.
  3. I went home early on Wednesday.
  4. Did you have a good week?

One short scene

You play yourself, on a Monday. I play someone from your team who stops at your desk. Your half is on the left and you get a minute to prepare it; my half stays hidden until we start, because in real life it would be.

Monday morning You vs. a colleague who wants the whole weekend
Quarter past nine on a Monday. You are at your desk and the coffee is not finished yet. Someone from your team stops by, sits on the edge of the desk, and asks how your weekend was.
You — at your desk
  • Say how it was in one sentence, before any detail.
  • Say three things you did, in the order you did them.
  • Say one thing you did not do.
  • Ask the same question back, then two more — and one of them has to start with What, Where or How.
  • End it: you have a call at half past nine.
Me — someone from your team
Hidden until the scene starts.
  • Friendly, a bit tired, in no hurry at all.
  • "Morning! How was your weekend?"
  • "Nice — where did you go?"
  • "Did you? What was that like?"
  • One answer given fast, with three verbs in it, so the repeat phrases have to come out.
  • One question dropped into the middle of your answer, to break the script.
