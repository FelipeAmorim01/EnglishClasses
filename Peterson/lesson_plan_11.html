Your story toolkit
Three blocks: what had already happened, how long it had been going on, and the words that make it a story.
1 · It had already started — the earlier past

A story is in the past. When you jump back to something even earlier, use had + the third form — the same form that comes after have: gone, seen, left. It's the same for every person: I had, she had, they had. Listen and repeat only: in speech had becomes a very short 'd — I'd, she'd, we'd — the same sound as last class's would. With a third form after it, it's had.

What you're saying The words Say it out loud
it was over when you got there had already … When I got there, the meeting had already started. The others had already left.
a moment before had just … I'd just sat down when the phone rang. She'd just gone out.
the first time I'd never … before I'd never flown before that trip. I'd never seen snow before.
a deadline in the story by the time … By the time I arrived, everyone had gone home. By the time she called, I'd fallen asleep.
the reason because … had … I was late because I'd missed the bus. She was angry because I'd forgotten her birthday.
asking Had you … before? Had you been there before? Had you met him before the meeting?
Everyone had went home.
Everyone had gone home.
After had, the third form — the one after have: gone, not went.
I never had seen the sea before.
I'd never seen the sea before.
never goes after had, just like I've never.
She said she finished the report.
She said she'd finished the report.
She said is already the past, and the report came before it — so had finished.
2 · I'd been waiting for an hour — how long, before it happened

Lesson 9's I've been working here for …, moved back into the past. had been + -ing says how long something had been going on when something else happened — or why someone was tired, wet or angry. The same verbs as lesson 9 never take -ing: know, like, be, and have for things you own — I'd known him for years.

What you're saying The words Say it out loud
how long, then something happened I'd been …ing for … when … I'd been waiting for an hour when the bus came. We'd been talking for ten minutes when the call dropped.
since when had been …ing since … She'd been working there since 2015 when they closed the office.
the reason you could see because … had been …ing He was tired because he'd been driving all night. The street was wet — it had been raining.
asking how long How long had you been …ing? How long had you been living there? How long had you been waiting?
know, like, be, have I'd known · I'd had I'd known him for years. I'd had the car for ten years when it broke down.
I was waiting for an hour when the bus came.
I'd been waiting for an hour when the bus came.
for an hour counts the time up to that moment — so had been …ing.
I'd been knowing him for years.
I'd known him for years.
know never takes -ing — not now, not then.
3 · Long story short — words that tell a story

These turn a list of sentences into a story people want to hear. Most of them start the sentence, with a comma after them.

What you're saying The words Say it out loud
nobody expected it all of a sudden · suddenly All of a sudden, the lights went out. Suddenly, everyone was looking at me.
bad, then worse to make matters worse I missed the bus, and to make matters worse, it started to rain.
you found out later it turned out (that) … It turned out that the meeting had been canceled. It turned out he was the new CEO!
good luck, bad luck luckily · unfortunately Luckily, I'd saved the file. Unfortunately, nobody had told me.
the surprise in the story the funny thing is … The funny thing is, I'd never met him before. The funny thing is, nobody noticed.
how it ended in the end · long story short In the end, we took a taxi. Long story short, I got the job.
At the end, we took a taxi.
In the end, we took a taxi.
in the end = after everything. at the end needs of: at the end of the day, at the end of the movie.
Eventually, I go to the gym.
Sometimes I go to the gym.
eventually means in the end, after a long time: Eventually, the bus came.
Suddenly, the lights were going out.
Suddenly, the lights went out.
The sudden thing is short — one moment. That's the past.
Language Tip
The meeting started vs. the meeting had started
Both are the past — the choice is about order. If you were there when it began, it's the past. If it began before you got there — it was already going, or already over — it's had + the third form. So ask: which happened first?
It started — you were there, then it began
when + past, past
  • When I sat down, the movie started.
  • When she called, I left. (her call came first)
  • When the boss came in, everyone stopped talking.
It had started — it began before you got there
when + past, had + third form
  • When I sat down, the movie had started.
  • When she called, I'd left. (I was already gone)
  • When the boss came in, everyone had stopped talking.
The trap

✗ When I got to the station, the train left.  →  ✓ When I got to the station, the train had left.

The first one says you watched it go. You got there, and then it left. If it was already gone — you missed it — it's had left. Add already and it's even clearer: the train had already left.

Both sides — say all four out loud
  1. When I got to the office, the meeting started — I was just in time.
  2. When I got to the office, the meeting had already started — I was late.
  3. When she said hello, I knew her voice — I'd met her years before.
  4. By the time we got to the restaurant, the kitchen had closed, so we went for a pizza.

A day that went wrong
You talk, I ask

Think of a real day when things went wrong — a trip, a day at work, getting somewhere on time. Tell me the story, and I'll ask questions as you go. The past for what happened, and had for anything earlier.

1 · The setting
When was it, and where were you? What were you doing when it started?
2 · Already wrong
What had already happened, or gone wrong, before you noticed? How long had you been waiting, or trying?
3 · What happened next
What did you do? Did anything happen all of a sudden — or make matters worse?
4 · In the end
How did it end? Did anything turn out to be different from what you thought?
