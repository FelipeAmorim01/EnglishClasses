Stage 02 of 04
Your back-then toolkit
Three blocks: what used to be true, the stories you tell about it, and what's normal for you now.
1 · I used to … — true then, not now

used to + a verb is for something that was true, or happened again and again, in the past — and isn't true now. It's only for the past: for a habit now, say usually. After did the -d goes: didn't use to, Did you use to …? Listen and repeat only: used to sounds like yoos-tuh, with an s, not a z — and use to sounds exactly the same.

What you're saying The words Say it out loud
true then, not now I used to … I used to live in Curitiba. I used to work in a bank.
not true then I didn't use to … I didn't use to like coffee. She didn't use to work on Fridays.
asking Did you use to …? Did you use to play football? Did you use to live near here?
a place that's changed There used to be … There used to be a cinema here. There used to be a lot of trees on my street.
it stopped … not anymore I used to go to the gym, but not anymore. I don't smoke anymore.
and now these days · nowadays These days, I work from home. Nowadays, everyone has a phone.
I use to wake up at six.
I usually wake up at six.
used to is only for the past. For something you do now, most days, it's usually.
I didn't used to like coffee.
I didn't use to like coffee.
did already says it's the past, so the -d goes — like I didn't work, never I didn't worked.
2 · we'd go, he'd cook — telling the story

When you tell a story about back then, English often starts with used to and then switches to would — 'd — for things that happened again and again. Only for actions: be, have, live, like and know stay with used to. And something that happened once is just the past. Listen and repeat only: we'd, he'd — the 'd is very short.

What you're saying The words Say it out loud
set the scene We used to …, and we'd … We used to live near the sea, and we'd swim every morning.
every year Every summer, we'd … Every summer, we'd go to the beach. We'd stay for a whole month.
someone's habit On Sundays, my dad would … On Sundays, my dad would cook. He'd always make too much food.
a normal day After school, we'd … After school, we'd play in the street. We wouldn't go home until dark.
a state, not an action used to be · used to have My grandmother used to have a farm. It used to be very quiet there.
it happened once the past One summer, we went to Bahia. It rained every day!
I would have a dog when I was a kid.
I used to have a dog when I was a kid.
have a dog isn't an action that happens again and again — it's a state. States take used to.
In 2010, I would go to Disney.
In 2010, I went to Disney.
One trip, one time — that's the past. would is only for again and again.
3 · I'm used to it — what's normal for you now

A different thing from block 1. be used to means it's normal for you — not strange, not hard. get used to is the change: it was strange, and it's becoming normal. After both comes a thing, or a verb with -ing. They work in every tense, including last class's: I've gotten used to it.

What you're saying The words Say it out loud
normal for me I'm used to … I'm used to the traffic. I'm used to working late.
still strange I'm not used to … I'm not used to the cold. I'm not used to getting up so early.
asking Are you used to …? Are you used to the new office? Are you used to working from home?
it's changing I'm getting used to … I'm getting used to my new boss. I'm slowly getting used to it.
it's changed I've gotten used to … I've gotten used to the noise. It took me a while to get used to it.
it won't change I can't get used to … I can't get used to this keyboard. I'll never get used to it.
I'm not used with the cold.
I'm not used to the cold.
It's always used to — never with.
I used to the noise now.
I'm used to the noise now.
Without am · is · are, it's block 1 — the past. Normal now needs the 'm.
I can't get use to it.
I can't get used to it.
You can't hear the -d, but it's always there: get used to, be used to.
Language Tip
I used to work vs. I'm used to working
They sound almost the same, and they mean very different things. I used to work is the past — it was true, and it stopped. I'm used to working is now — it's normal for you. So look at what comes before used to: nothing, or am · is · are?
I used to work — true then, not now
used to + verb
  • I used to work in an office.
  • She used to live alone.
  • Did you use to get up early?
I'm used to working — normal for me now
am · is · are used to + …ing · a thing
  • I'm used to working from home.
  • She's used to living alone.
  • Are you used to getting up early?
The trap

✗ I'm used to work from home.  →  ✓ I'm used to working from home.

With am · is · are in front, the next verb takes -ing. Here to works like to in I'm going to the office — a thing comes after it, so a verb has to become a thing: working. With nothing in front, it's the past and the verb stays plain: I used to work. And for a habit now, it's neither — it's I usually work.

Both sides — say all four out loud
  1. I used to work in an office, but now I'm used to working from home.
  2. She used to live in Bahia, so she's used to the heat.
  3. I didn't use to drink coffee, but now I'm used to three cups a day.
  4. Are you used to the new system yet? — Not really. The old one used to be easier.

Where you grew up
You talk, I ask

Think about the place you grew up, and what a normal week was like there. I'll ask questions as you go — used to and would for back then, and I'm used to for now.

2 · A normal week
What you'd do after school, and on weekends. What would your family do on Sundays?
3 · One time
One day you still remember from back then — what happened?
4 · And now
What's different where you live now? What did you have to get used to, and what are you still not used to?
