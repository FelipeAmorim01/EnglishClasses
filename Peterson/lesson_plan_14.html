Stage 02 of 04
Your verb toolkit
Three blocks: -ing or to, after a preposition, and verb + person + verb.
1 · I enjoy working, I decided to stay — -ing or to

When one verb follows another, the second one takes -ing or to — and the first verb decides which. There's no rule you can see, so learn them in groups. One help: many to verbs look forward — decide, plan, hope, want — to something not done yet. With like, love, hate and start, both are fine.

What you're saying The words Say it out loud
what you like or don't enjoy · don't mind · can't stand + -ing I enjoy cooking. I don't mind waiting. I can't stand driving in traffic.
again and again · done · not doing keep · finish · avoid + -ing I keep forgetting his name. I finished writing the report. I avoid driving at night.
a plan or a choice decide · plan · hope · want + to I decided to stay. We're planning to move. I hope to see you soon.
it worked · yes · no manage · agree · refuse + to I managed to finish on time. She agreed to wait. He refused to help.
both are fine like · love · hate · start + -ing or to I like cooking. I like to cook. It started raining. It started to rain.
I enjoy to travel.
I enjoy traveling.
enjoy always takes -ing.
Do you mind to wait a minute?
Do you mind waiting a minute?
mind takes -ing — in questions too.
We decided going by car.
We decided to go by car.
decide looks forward, so it takes to.
2 · I'm looking forward to seeing you — after a preposition

After a preposition — in, at, of, about, before, after, without — a verb always takes -ing. Careful with looking forward to: that to is a preposition too, the same as in I'm used to working. If you can put a noun after it — looking forward to the weekend — the verb takes -ing.

What you're saying The words Say it out loud
how you feel about it interested in · good at · tired of I'm interested in learning Spanish. I'm good at fixing things. I'm tired of waiting.
first · then · not before · after · without Before leaving, I check the windows. After eating, I go for a walk. He left without saying goodbye.
a plan in your head thinking about · thinking of I'm thinking about changing jobs. We're thinking of moving to the coast.
an idea How about …? · instead of How about going to the beach? Let's walk instead of taking the bus.
something good is coming looking forward to I'm looking forward to seeing you. I'm looking forward to the weekend.
I'm thinking to change jobs.
I'm thinking about changing jobs.
thinking about + -ing — never thinking to.
Before to leave, call me.
Before leaving, call me.
Or Before you leave, call me. Never before to.
I'm looking forward to see you.
I'm looking forward to seeing you.
Here to is a preposition, so -ing.
3 · She made me wait — verb + person + verb

Some verbs take a person and then another verb. After want, need, expect and would like, the second verb takes to: I want you to come. After make and let, there's no to. And never that after want.

What you're saying The words Say it out loud
what you want from someone want · need · expect + person + to I want you to come. I need you to sign this. They expect us to work late.
politely I'd like + person + to I'd like you to meet Ana. I'd like you to check this for me.
no choice make + person + verb My boss made me stay late. Sad movies make me cry.
it's OK — permission let + person + verb My parents let me stay out late. Let me help you.
helping help + person + verb Can you help me carry this? She helped me find an apartment.
I want that you come to the party.
I want you to come to the party.
want + person + to. No that.
My boss made me to stay late.
My boss made me stay late.
No to after make + person.
They don't let us to use our phones.
They don't let us use our phones.
No to after let. With allow, you need it: they don't allow us to use.
Language Tip
I stopped smoking vs. I stopped to smoke
Same verb, opposite meaning. With -ing, the activity is over — you don't do it anymore. With to, you stop what you're doing so you can do something else: the to tells you why you stopped.
It's over — stop + -ing
stop + verb-ing
  • I stopped smoking five years ago.
  • Stop talking — the movie's starting.
  • It's stopped raining. Let's go.
Why you stopped — stop + to
stop + to + verb
  • We stopped to get gas on the way.
  • I stopped to talk to a friend in the street.
  • She stopped to answer the phone.
The trap

✗ "I stopped to smoke last year — it was hard."  →  ✓ "I stopped smoking last year."

With to, you stopped something else so you could smoke — the opposite of what you mean. Ask yourself: is it over? Then it's -ing. quit and give up are the same, and they only take -ing: I quit smoking.

Both sides — say all four out loud
  1. I stopped drinking coffee after six — I sleep much better.
  2. We stopped to buy water on the way to the beach.
  3. Can you stop calling me at work?
  4. Halfway up the hill, we stopped to rest.
