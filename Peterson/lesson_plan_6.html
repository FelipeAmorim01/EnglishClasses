Calls at work

The conversation this whole lesson is for, and you have it for real — nothing here is for repeating. I ask you about calls at work, from the first row. You answer by finishing one of the second row with something true, and keep it going with the third.

1 · I ask you
How many calls do you have on a normal day, and who with? Do you prefer a call, a video call or a message? When did a call last go wrong?
2 · You answer — finish one with something true
On a normal day I have about … calls, mostly with … I'd rather …, because … The hardest part is …, especially when … Last week …, so in the end …
3 · You keep it going
What about you? Do you …? Does that happen to you a lot? What do you do when …? How do you deal with …?

2 · pick up, call back, hold on — the verbs of a call

A call runs on two-word verbs. With a name or a thing, the second word can go before it or after it: pick up the phone, call Ana back. With it, him, her, you, me or them, the small word always goes in the middle: pick it up, call her back.

What's happening The words Say it out loud
answering pick up She never picks up before ten. If it rings, can you pick it up?
ending the call hang up Don't hang up — I'm still here. I'll hang up and call you again.
calling again later call back Can I call you back in ten minutes? I'll call you back after lunch.
waiting hold on Hold on a second — I'll just get a pen. Can you hold on?
connecting someone put through Hold on, I'll put you through to Ana. Could you put me through to sales?
when it goes wrong break up · get cut off Sorry, you're breaking up. I think we got cut off — I'll call you back.
I'll call back you after lunch.
I'll call you back after lunch.
you, me, him, her, it, them go in the middle: pick it up, put you through.

3 · Taking a message — tell, ask, say

To pass on a message, tell and ask go straight to the person — tell her, ask him, no to in between. Then to and the verb, for what they should do: ask him to call me. For what they shouldn't do: not to. say never has the person straight after it: she said it's urgent.

What you're doing The words Say it out loud
offering Can I take a message? She's not at her desk. Can I take a message? Can I tell her who's calling?
leaving one Could you tell her …? Could you tell her I called? Could you tell her it's about the contract?
asking for something ask … to … Could you ask him to call me back? Could you ask him to call before five?
asking them not to ask … not to … Ask them not to start without me, and ask them not to send it yet.
passing it on She said … Ana called. She said it's urgent, and she said she's at her desk until six.
promising I'll let him know I'll let him know you called, and I'll let him know it's urgent.
Can you say him to call me?
Can you tell him to call me?
tell takes a person: tell him, tell me. say doesn't: say something, say that …

No to between ask and the person. The to comes once, before the verb.

4 · Does Thursday work? — finding a time, and moving it

To find a time, ask what works for the other person, and say what doesn't work for you — with a reason. To change it: push it back is later, bring it forward is earlier, and move it to … is just different. The small word goes in the middle again: push it back, never push back it.

What you're doing The words Say it out loud
asking Does … work for you? Does Thursday work for you? Does ten work, or is it too early?
saying when I'm tied up … · I'm free … I'm tied up all morning, but I'm free after two — I'm free until five.
saying no I can't do … I can't do Thursday — I'm in Brasília. I can do Friday, though.
later push it back Could we push it back to four? Could we push it back an hour?
earlier bring it forward Could we bring it forward to Monday? If we bring it forward, we'll have more time.
closing Let's say … Let's say Thursday at ten, then. I'll send you an invite.
Can we anticipate the meeting?
Can we bring the meeting forward?
anticipate means expect: We anticipate a busy week. Earlier is bring forward; later is push back.
Can we change the meeting for Friday?
Can we move the meeting to Friday?
change … for means swap one thing for another. A new day takes move … to.
I can't on Thursday.
I can't do Thursday.
can't needs a verb after it: I can't do Thursday, or I can't make it on Thursday.
Language Tip
by Friday vs. until Friday
Both answer when?, but they mean different things. by is a deadline: something happens once, at that time or before it. until is a stretch of time: something keeps going, and stops at that time. So ask yourself — does it happen once, or does it keep going?
by — a deadline, once
it happens once + by + the time
  • Send me the report by Friday.
  • I'll be home by seven.
  • We need an answer by the 20th.
until — it keeps going, then stops
it keeps going + until + the time
  • I'm in the office until six.
  • The offer is open until Friday.
  • Hold on until I find a pen.
The trap

✗ I need the numbers until three.  →  ✓ I need the numbers by three.

Getting the numbers happens once — that's a deadline. With until, it would mean you need them all the time from now to three, and not after that. One more thing: after a negative, until means not before — It won't be ready until Friday means Friday, and not earlier.

Both sides — say all four out loud
  1. Please send me the numbers by three.
  2. I'm on a call until four — can I call you back?
  3. The offer is open until Friday, so we need an answer by Thursday.
  4. Could you ask her to reply by tonight? I'm at my desk until seven.

A message for Ana
You and me, on the phone

You're at work, and you answer your colleague Ana's phone — she's out until three. I'm calling from another company, and I talk fast. Take a message, and check every letter and number: Ana has to be able to call me back.

1 · Who's calling
Get my name and my company — and how to spell them.
2 · How to reach me

3 · The message
Find out what I want Ana to do, and by when.
4 · Our meeting
You and I are meeting on Wednesday at ten, and I'll ask to move it. You're tied up all Thursday morning, but you're free after two — and all day Friday.
