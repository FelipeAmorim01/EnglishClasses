Stage 02 of 06
Real or fake?
In Read and Select, fake words are built to look real. Many of them are a real root with the wrong ending, or a real word spelled wrong. Learn the patterns and the fakes start to stand out.
How fakes are made
Wrong ending
A real root with a suffix that doesn't belong to it.
comfort → comfortable, not comfortment
Misspelled real word
One letter missing, doubled or swapped. It's still a fake.
necessary is real, neccessary is fake
Sounds English, means nothing
Normal letter patterns, but you can't give it a meaning or use it in a sentence.
brintle, glimber, plome
Pick only what you know
Picking a fake word costs you points. If you can't use it in a sentence, leave it.
Test: "Can I say a sentence with this word?"

Stage 03 of 06
Missing letters
In Read and Complete, the first and last sentences are complete, and many words in between are cut in half. The grammar around each word tells you how it has to end.
Three steps
Do it in this order
  1. Read the first sentence. It's complete and tells you the topic. Spend 10 seconds on it.
  2. Fill in the small words first: the, of, to, in, and, is, that. They are quick points.
  3. Then use grammar for the rest. Look at the word just before the gap and ask: noun, verb or adjective? Singular or plural? Past or present?
Grammar clues
If you see… The word probably… Example
many, several, two, these is plural: -s, -es, -ies several studies
he, she, it, a single noun takes -s in the present air quality improves
yesterday, last year, has, was ends in -ed, -en or -ing what they studied
the, a, his, their is a noun or an adjective their decision
more, than, very is an adjective, maybe -er harder than
after a verb may be an adverb in -ly improves quickly

Stage 04 of 06
Spelling traps
These words are misspelled all the time, even by native speakers. On the test, the wrong spelling can show up as a fake word, and there's no autocorrect to save you.
EPick the right spelling
2:00
In each pair, click the correct spelling. Any you miss go on your personal spelling list.
  • i before e, except after c: believe, achieve, but receive, ceiling.
  • Double consonant when a short stressed syllable ends in one consonant: begin → beginning, occur → occurred.
  • Hidden letters you don't hear: environment, government, February, Wednesday.
  • Final e drops before -ing but not always before -ment: argue → argument, but manage → management.
Your spelling list

Keep a separate list of every word you misspell this month: from today, from your sprints, and from my corrections. Write each one correctly three times, then use it in a sentence. Bring the list to every class.
