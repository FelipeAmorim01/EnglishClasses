Stage 02 of 04
How the test works
About one hour, done at home on your computer. The test is adaptive: when you get questions right, the next ones get harder.
Your score
10–160
Overall score, in steps of 5. Results usually arrive in about two days.
120+
Our target, so a bad day still gets you over 110.

You also get four subscores (reading, writing, listening, speaking) and four combined ones: literacy (reading + writing), comprehension (reading + listening), conversation (speaking + listening) and production (speaking + writing).

Six rules that move your score
Use all the time
Speaking and writing are scored on length, range and accuracy. A short, safe answer scores low.
Keep talking until the last 5 seconds.
Spelling counts
Read and Complete, Fill in the Blanks and Listen and Type all need exact spelling.
There's no autocorrect on test day.
Hard means good
The test adapts. If the questions feel hard, it usually means you're doing well.
Don't panic when it gets difficult.
Never leave it blank
A guess can earn partial credit. An empty answer earns nothing.
Write something, even if you're unsure.
Test-day rules
Break one and your result can be cancelled
The test is watched and recorded. These are the usual rules. Read the official rules page again before you book.
  1. Be alone in a quiet room with the door closed.
  2. No headphones or earbuds, and keep your ears visible.
  3. Keep your eyes on the screen. Looking away often is flagged.
  4. No notes, paper, phone or second screen, unless the phone is your required second camera.
  5. Have your passport or ID ready before you start.
  6. Don't read the questions out loud, except on the speaking tasks.

GInteractive Speaking
35 s each · no prep
A conversation about starting college. You hear each question once and answer straight away. Each answer gets 35 seconds, and there's no time to prepare.
  1. Why did you choose this college, or this course?
  2. What do you think will be the hardest part of your first year?
  3. How are you getting ready for that?
  4. Tell me about a time you had to get used to something new.
  5. What would you miss most if you studied far from home?
  6. Some people say students should work for a year before college. What do you think?
The questions are read aloud, so keep them hidden while you answer.

Before next class
Practice test
Take the second free practice test on englishtest.duolingo.com. Do it like the real thing: one sitting, a quiet room, no notes, no pausing. Send me your scores.
Word list
Start your word list: 10 new words a day, each one in a sentence of your own. Type them yourself, with autocorrect off.
Writing

A 5-minute writing sprint, once a day. It trains your writing and your typing speed together, just like the Interactive Writing task.

  1. Pick one prompt below. Turn autocorrect off.
  2. Set a 5-minute timer and type without stopping. Aim for 100 words or more.
  3. When the timer ends, take 1 minute to reread it and fix mistakes.
  4. Write down your word count, so you can see it go up.
  5. Send me two of your sprints each week.
Prompts
  1. Describe a place in your city that you would show a visitor. Why that place?
  2. Some people prefer to study alone, others in groups. Which do you prefer, and why?
  3. Write about a mistake that taught you something important.
  4. Should universities make attendance in class mandatory? Give reasons.
  5. Describe a typical weekday in your life. What would you change about it?
  6. Is it better to have one very good friend or many friends? Explain.
  7. What technology could you not live without? Why?
