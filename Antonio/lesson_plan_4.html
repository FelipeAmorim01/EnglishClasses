Stage 02 of 06
Listen and type
You hear one sentence and type exactly what you heard. You get one minute, and you can play it up to three times: the first time and two replays. Every word counts, including the small ones.
Why words go missing
Small words go quiet
In fast speech, a, the, of, to, and, have shrink to a tiny sound. They are still in the sentence, so they must be in your answer.
a cup of tea → "a cup-uh tea"
Contractions
Type what you hear. If you hear "I'd", type I'd, not I would. Then check what it stands for, because it changes the next word.
I'd known (I had) · I'd go (I would)
Endings you barely hear
The -s and -ed at the end of a word are short, and the next word often swallows them. Grammar tells you if they're there.
She works · we walked to · two students
Use the three plays
Play 1: just listen, then type everything you can. Play 2: fill the holes. Play 3: read your sentence while it plays.
listen → fill → check
The page reads each sentence with your computer's voice. If there's no voice, or it sounds wrong, the sentence is read aloud instead.

    Stage 04 of 06
    Interactive listening
    You read a situation, then follow a conversation one line at a time. You hear each line only once and choose the best reply, and you see straight away if you were right. At the end you write a short summary of the whole conversation. The exact parts can vary on the real test, so check them on the official practice test.
    Three steps
    Do it in this order
    1. Read the situation first. Who are you, who is the other person, and why are you talking? A professor and a friend need different replies.
    2. Answer the last thing they said. The right reply answers the question or moves the plan forward. Watch out for options that repeat a word you heard but mean something else.
    3. Summarize in three sentences. Who talked and why, what they said, and how it ended. Use your own words.
    Prep sites say the summary gets about 75 seconds. That isn't on Duolingo's own pages, so check it on the official practice test.

    Before next class
    Listening

    Watch or listen to one short English video or podcast every day (5–10 minutes). Each day:

    1. Pick two sentences and write them down exactly as you hear them.
    2. Play each one three times at most, like the test.
    3. Check with the subtitles or transcript, and circle every word you missed.

    Send me two of your sentences with the circles.

    Word list
    Keep going with 10 new words a day, with the word they usually go with. New rule: play each word in an online dictionary and listen to it before you write it down. Autocorrect off.
    Spelling list
    Add the words you missed today and in your daily sentences. Every day, cover the list and write five of them from memory.
    Writing

    Keep the daily 5-minute sprint with two linking words. New rule: when you finish, set a 75-second timer and summarize your own answer in three sentences. Send me two sprints with their summaries this week.

    New prompts

    2. Should university lectures be recorded and put online?
    3. What is the best way to ask a teacher for help?
    4. Do you prefer working alone or in a group? Why?
    5. Describe a podcast, song or video you have listened to many times.
    6. Is it rude to use your phone while someone is talking to you?
    7. What would you do if you missed an important deadline?
