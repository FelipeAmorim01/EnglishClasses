Stage 02 of 06
Write about the photo
You see a picture and get one minute to write about it. One simple sentence scores low. Two or three rich sentences, with details and a guess, score high.
Three sentences in one minute
The photo frame, in writing
The same frame you use for speaking. One minute is enough for the first three steps.
  1. This picture shows… (overview) Where it is and what is happening, in one sentence.
  2. In the foreground / On the left, there is… (2–3 details) Join two details in one sentence.
  3. It looks like… / They might be… (guess the story) Why they are there, or what happens next.
Three facts per sentence
Who, doing what, and where. Never stop at "There is a woman."
A young woman is working on her laptop next to the window.
Join with while, which, so
One longer sentence with a linking word shows more grammar than two short ones.
…ordering at the counter while the barista makes coffee.
Start each sentence differently
Not "There is… There is… There is…". Begin with a place, a person or a guess.
On the table… · In the background… · It looks like…
Keep 10 seconds to check
There's no autocorrect. Check the verb endings, the plurals and any word you're unsure how to spell.
a woman are → a woman is

Stage 03 of 06
Interactive writing
You answer a written prompt in 5 minutes. Then a follow-up question on the same topic appears, and you get 3 more minutes. Use all the time: longer, well-linked answers score higher.
Answer → reason → example → close
The opinion frame, in writing
Four parts, one or two sentences each. The optional line adds range when you have time.
  1. In my opinion,… (clear answer)
  2. The main reason is that…
  3. For example, when I…
  4. On the other hand,… (optional, adds range)
  5. So overall, I believe…
Answer in sentence one
The reader should know your side after the first line. Don't repeat the question first.
In my opinion, students should…
A real example
A short story from your life is the easiest way to add length, past tenses and detail.
For example, when I was at school…
The follow-up is new
It asks something different about the same topic. Answer it directly. Don't copy your first answer.
If I were a teacher, I would…
Last 30 seconds: check
Stop adding ideas and read it once: verb endings, a/an/the, plurals and spelling.
He don't → He doesn't

Some people think students should have homework every day. Others think it isn't necessary. What do you think?
Set the clock to 5:00 and write with the frame. When it beeps, show the follow-up question and set the clock to 3:00.
0 words
In my opinion, students should have some homework, but not every single day. The main reason is that homework helps you remember what you learned in class, but too much of it leaves no time for rest, sport or family. For example, when I was in secondary school, we had homework in almost every subject, and I often stayed up until midnight to finish it. As a result, I was tired in class the next morning and learned less, not more. On the other hand, I understand why teachers give homework, because one lesson is not enough to practise a new skill properly. So overall, I believe two or three short assignments a week are much more useful than a long list every day.
Follow-up question
If you were a teacher, how much homework would you give, and what kind?
0 words
If I were a teacher, I would give two short pieces of homework a week, and each one would take about thirty minutes. Instead of long exercises from the book, I would ask students to use what we learned in real life, such as writing an email, recording a short video or interviewing someone in their family. In my view, this kind of homework is more interesting, so students are more likely to actually do it.

Stage 04 of 06
Link it, then check it
Linking words and different sentence starts make your writing read better. A quick check for your usual mistakes keeps the grammar score up. Today you start a log of those mistakes.
Mistakes to check for
Check Wrong Right
Verb after he / she / it She work in a bank. She works in a bank.
Plural after many, two, a lot of many student many students
Past tense with last, ago, yesterday Last year I go to London. Last year I went to London.
Have + past participle I have saw it. I have seen it.
a / an before jobs and vowel sounds She is engineer. She is an engineer.
The preposition after the word It depends of the weather. It depends on the weather.
Two sentences joined by a comma I was tired, I went to bed. I was tired, so I went to bed.

Wrap-up: what's next
Before next class
Interactive Writing

One timed Interactive Writing every other day, typed with autocorrect off:

  1. Set a timer for 5:00 and answer the prompt with the opinion frame.
  2. Only then read the follow-up, and give it 3:00.
  3. Keep the last 30 seconds of each part to check.
  4. Count your words and send me each one.
Prompt, then follow-up
  1. Should cities ban cars from the centre? · Follow-up: How do you usually travel around your city, and why?
  2. Is it a good idea to have a part-time job while you study? · Follow-up: What did you learn from a job or a responsibility you had?
  3. Are online friends as real as friends you meet in person? · Follow-up: How did you meet your best friend?
  4. Should tourists pay extra to visit popular places? · Follow-up: Describe a place you visited that was too crowded.
Error log
After every piece of writing, add your mistakes to the log: the mistake, the correct version, the rule. Bring it to next class: we'll find your three most repeated mistakes and fix them.
Write About the Photo
On the days without Interactive Writing, take a photo from your phone and give yourself one minute: three sentences with the frame, one of them joined with while or which.
Speaking

Keep one 90-second recording a day: 20 seconds, three keywords, record, listen back, record again. New rule: use one linking word from each group (adding, contrast, cause, example). Send me one recording.

Prompts
  1. Describe the place where you usually study.
  2. Is it better to save money or to spend it on experiences?
  3. Talk about a change you would like to see in your city.
  4. Retell one of your Interactive Writing answers, without reading it.
Word list & listening
Keep both going: 10 new words a day with the word they go with, and one video or podcast a day with two sentences exactly as heard. New rule: use two words from your list in each Interactive Writing.
Practice test
