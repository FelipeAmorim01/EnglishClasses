Stage 02 of 06
Speak about the photo
You see a picture, get 20 seconds to prepare, then speak for 30 to 90 seconds. Short, safe answers score low, so use the photo frame and keep talking until the time is nearly over.
The photo frame
Four steps, in this order
  1. This picture shows… (overview) One sentence: where it is and what is happening.
  2. In the foreground / On the left, there is… (2–3 details) People, what they're doing, the objects around them.
  3. It looks like… / They might be… (guess the story) Why they're there, what happened before, what happens next.
  4. This reminds me of… (personal link) A real memory or a wish. This is the easiest way to fill the last 30 seconds.
Actions take -ing
For what people are doing in the picture, use the present continuous.
Two people are sitting… · a child is flying…
Never "I don't know"
You don't need to be right about the story. Guess, and say it's a guess.
It could be… · Maybe they're… · I'd say…
Go round twice
Finished the frame at 0:50? Go back to step 2: one more detail, then one more guess.
Another thing I notice is…
Bigger words, same idea
Range of vocabulary is part of the score. Swap one safe word in every answer.
very happy → delighted

Stage 03 of 06
Read / listen, then speak
You read a prompt (or hear it), get 20 seconds to prepare, then speak for 30 to 90 seconds. The 20 seconds decide the answer: spend them on three keywords, not on full sentences.
Using the 20 seconds
Three keywords
One for your answer, one for your reason, one for your example. That's your whole plan.
no · freedom · my old school
No full sentences
Twenty seconds is enough to write three words, not one good sentence. The sentences come when you speak.
✗ In my opinion, students should…
Pick a side fast
Decide in five seconds. Choose the side you have the best example for, even if you're not sure what you think.
city or countryside? → city
Bullets are a plan
If the prompt has bullet points, write one keyword per bullet and answer them in order.
what · why · how → 3 keywords
The opinion frame
For prompts that ask what you think. Each line is one or two sentences.
  1. In my opinion,… (clear answer)
  2. The main reason is that…
  3. For example, when I…
  4. On the other hand,… (optional, adds range)
  5. So overall, I believe…

ERead, Then Speak
20 s prep · 1:30
Talk about a place in your city that you would recommend to a visitor.
  • What is the place?
  • What can people do there?
  • Why do you recommend it?
Press Start, type one keyword per bullet in the 20 seconds, then speak.
Your three keywords
One place in my city that I'd recommend to any visitor is the walk along the river, especially in the late afternoon. It's about three kilometres long, and there are trees, small cafés and benches all the way. People go there to walk, run, ride their bikes, or just sit and watch the boats go past. At the weekend, there's usually live music near the old bridge, and you can buy cheap street food from the stalls. The main reason I recommend it is that it shows you how people in my city really live. Tourist attractions can feel a bit artificial, but the river walk is full of local families and friends. For example, the last time my cousins visited, we spent a whole evening there, and they said it was the best part of their trip. So if you only have one day in my city, I'd definitely go there.
FListen, Then Speak
20 s prep · 1:30
This time you hear the prompt instead of reading it. Play it once, then press Start and write your three keywords. Use the opinion frame.
Some people say that young people spend too much time on their phones. Do you agree or disagree? Explain why.
Your three keywords
In my opinion, it's true that many young people spend too much time on their phones, but it isn't always a bad thing. The main reason is that a lot of that time is spent on things that are actually useful, like studying, reading the news or keeping in touch with family who live far away. For example, when I was preparing for this exam, I used my phone every day to learn vocabulary and listen to podcasts in English. On the other hand, social media can be a real problem, because it's very easy to scroll for hours without noticing. Sometimes I put my phone in another room when I need to concentrate, and it really helps. So overall, I believe the problem isn't the phone itself, but the way we use it.
How many times the prompt plays can change. Check it on the official practice test.

Listen back and fix
The fastest way to improve a spoken answer is to hear it. Under every recording, the strip shows where you spoke and where you stopped: click it to jump to that moment.
What to listen for
On the recording Fix Say instead
A long silence (orange on the strip) Buy a second with a phrase, then go back to the frame. Let me think about that for a second.
Starting a word twice: "the the", "I I think" Slow down a little and finish the sentence. Don't go back. I think…
"Um" and "uh" every few words Pause silently, or use one linking word. Well,… · Also,…
The same word again and again Swap it for a stronger one (drill G). very big → spacious
Stopping before 1:00 Go round the frame again: a detail, a reason, an example. Another thing I notice is…
Every sentence starts the same Change the start: a place, a guess, a linking word. On the left… · It looks like…

Wrap-up: what's next
Before next class
Speaking

Record one 90-second answer every day. Each day:

  1. Give yourself 20 seconds and write three keywords.
  2. Record for 1:30 with your phone, or with any recorder on this page (Save keeps the file).
  3. Listen back. Count your long pauses and repeated words, and find one safe word to swap.
  4. Record it once more with those fixes.

Send me two recordings this week, a first and a second take.

Prompts, one a day
  1. Describe a photo from your phone, with the photo frame.
  2. Retell a video or podcast you watched today.
  3. Should students be allowed to use their phones in class?
  4. Talk about your favourite place to eat. What do you order? Why do you like it?
  5. Is it better to have a few close friends or many friends?
  6. Describe a trip you remember well.
  7. How will you celebrate after the exam?
Word list
Keep going with 10 new words a day, with the word they usually go with, listened to in a dictionary first. New rule: use two words from your list in each day's recording.
Listening
Keep the daily video or podcast, two sentences exactly as heard. On prompt 2 days, retell it out loud as your recording.
Writing
Three 5-minute sprints this week, autocorrect off, with two linking words. New rule: write about the prompt you recorded the day before, and use at least one word you didn't say out loud. Send me one sprint.
Practice test
If you still haven't taken the second practice test, take it before Tuesday, like the real thing, and send me the score. Listen to how the speaking tasks are timed.
